Why phishing matters

Phishing can lead to credential theft, payment fraud and loss of personal information.

0001

Credential phishing

A fake login page attempts to collect usernames and passwords.

Verify the domain.

0002

Banking phishing

A fraudulent page imitates a bank or payment provider.

Use the official banking application.

0003

Shopping phishing

Fake stores or offers attempt to collect payment information.

Check the merchant independently.

0004

Account verification scam

A message claims an account must be verified immediately.

Navigate to the service independently.

0005

Delivery scam

A fake parcel notification asks for payment or details.

Check delivery status through the official courier.

0006

Prize scam

A message claims that the user won a reward.

Unexpected prizes deserve caution.

0007

Job phishing

Fake recruiters request documents, fees or credentials.

Verify the organization.

0008

Social-media phishing

Attackers imitate social platforms or account warnings.

Open the platform directly.

0009

Email phishing

A deceptive email uses urgency or authority.

Inspect sender and destination.

0010

Smishing

Phishing is delivered through SMS or messaging apps.

Treat unexpected links as untrusted.

0011

Spear phishing

A targeted attack uses personalized information.

Verify unusual requests separately.

0012

QR phishing

A QR code redirects to a fraudulent destination.

Preview the destination first.

Security indicators

No single indicator is a final verdict.

0001

URL Length

Very long URLs can sometimes hide suspicious paths or parameters.

Length alone is not proof of phishing.

0002

HTTPS

HTTPS encrypts communication between browser and server.

HTTPS does not prove that a site is trustworthy.

0003

IP Address

A raw IP address can be unusual for a public login page.

Treat unexpected IP-based links cautiously.

0004

@ Symbol

An @ symbol can contribute to misleading URL structures.

Inspect the actual hostname.

0005

Hyphen Count

Hyphenated domains can sometimes imitate recognizable names.

Consider this with other indicators.

0006

Dot Count

Multiple dots can make a URL difficult to interpret.

Identify the registered domain.

0007

Subdomains

Deceptive subdomains can contain brand-like words.

Read the hostname carefully.

0008

Suspicious Words

Words such as login, verify, reward or update may appear in deceptive links.

Words are contextual signals.

0009

Query Length

Long query strings can contain many parameters.

Inspect unexpected parameters.

0010

Path Length

Long paths can hide the meaningful destination.

Review the complete URL.

0011

Domain Age

A newly registered domain can be an additional caution signal.

Domain age is not a verdict.

0012

Redirects

Unexpected redirects can hide the final destination.

Verify the final destination.

0013

Punycode

Internationalized domains can create visually confusing names.

Inspect normalized hostnames.

0014

Port Number

Non-standard ports can be legitimate but deserve context.

A port alone is not proof.

0015

Fragment

Fragments can contain page state or navigation.

Treat them as one part of analysis.

Safer browsing checklist

Use these habits before clicking unexpected links.

0001

Practice 1

Check the complete domain before entering sensitive information.

0002

Practice 2

Do not assume HTTPS means a website is legitimate.

0003

Practice 3

Avoid unexpected links in messages and emails.

0004

Practice 4

Use official applications for sensitive services.

0005

Practice 5

Keep browsers and operating systems updated.

0006

Practice 6

Use unique passwords and enable multi-factor authentication.

0007

Practice 7

Be cautious of urgent requests that create fear or pressure.

0008

Practice 8

Verify unusual payment requests independently.

0009

Practice 9

Do not install software from unsolicited links.

0010

Practice 10

Report suspicious messages through official channels.

0011

Practice 11

If you clicked a suspicious link, stop before entering information.

0012

Practice 12

If credentials were exposed, change the password through the official service.

0013

Practice 13

For financial exposure, contact the institution through an official channel.

0014

Practice 14

Monitor important accounts for unexpected activity.

0015

Practice 15

Teach family and colleagues how to inspect URLs.
